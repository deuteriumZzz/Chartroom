# Enclosed Space Entry

**Document:** SMS-EN-04 · **Revision:** 3 · **Date:** 2026-09-10 · **Fleet:** Chartroom Demo Fleet

> Synthetic document for the Chartroom demo. Not an operative procedure and not a substitute for a company SMS.

## 1. Purpose
This procedure prevents injuries and fatalities during entry into enclosed spaces, where the atmosphere may be oxygen-deficient, toxic or flammable.

## 2. Scope
Applies to cargo and ballast tanks, void spaces, cofferdams, chain lockers, duct keels and any other space with limited openings and poor natural ventilation.

## 3. Responsibilities
- The Master authorises every entry by signing the permit.
- The responsible officer tests the atmosphere and briefs the entry team.
- The standby attendant stays at the entrance for the whole duration of the entry.

## 4. Procedure
### 4.1 Permit to enter
No one enters an enclosed space without a valid permit. A permit is valid for one work shift and never longer than 8 hours. It is cancelled if conditions change or ventilation stops.

### 4.2 Ventilation and atmosphere testing
Ventilate the space before testing. Test the atmosphere at the upper, middle and lower levels with a calibrated multi-gas detector. Entry is allowed only when oxygen is 20.8–21.0 %, flammable gas is below 1 % LEL, H2S is below 5 ppm and CO is below 25 ppm.

### 4.3 During the entry
Every person inside carries a personal gas monitor. The attendant checks communication with the team at least every 15 minutes and orders everyone out if any monitor alarms.

### 4.4 Rescue
The attendant never enters the space to perform a rescue. The attendant raises the alarm, and the rescue team enters wearing breathing apparatus, with a rescue harness and stretcher prepared.

## 5. Drills and records
Enclosed space entry and rescue drills are held at least every two months. Completed permits are kept on board for 12 months.
