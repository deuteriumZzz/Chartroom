# Bunkering Operations

**Document:** SMS-EN-11 · **Revision:** 2 · **Date:** 2026-05-18 · **Fleet:** Chartroom Demo Fleet

> Synthetic document for the Chartroom demo. Not an operative procedure and not a substitute for a company SMS.

## 1. Purpose
This procedure ensures that fuel is received safely, in the ordered quantity and without pollution.

## 2. Responsibilities
- The Chief Engineer is in charge of the bunkering operation.
- The duty engineer monitors the manifold and the tank levels.
- The officer of the watch keeps the deck watch, checks the moorings and controls access to the bunker station.

## 3. Preparation
### 3.1 Pre-transfer agreement
Before connecting, the Chief Engineer and the supplier agree in writing on the grade and quantity, the maximum pumping rate, the communication channel and the emergency stop signal. The bunker checklist SMS-F-31 is completed and signed by both parties.

### 3.2 Pollution prevention
All deck scuppers are plugged, drip trays are empty, and SOPEP equipment (absorbents, shovels, empty drums) is placed at the manifold. No tank may be filled above 90 % of its capacity.

## 4. Transfer
### 4.1 Start
Pumping starts at a low rate. The rate is raised to the agreed maximum only after the duty engineer confirms that fuel is entering the correct tank and there are no leaks.

### 4.2 Topping off
When a tank approaches 80 %, the ship asks the supplier to reduce the rate. Tanks are switched before they reach the 90 % limit.

### 4.3 Emergency stop
Anyone who sees a leak or an overflow gives the agreed stop signal. Pumping stops immediately and restarts only with the Chief Engineer's permission.

## 5. Completion and records
After the transfer, the hose is drained and blanked before disconnection. A representative fuel sample is sealed, labelled and kept on board for at least 12 months. The quantity received is recorded in the Oil Record Book and checked against the bunker delivery note.
